Before you donate to any online fundraising campaign, take two minutes to verify it's legitimate. Check for a platform verification badge, review the documents uploaded, look at the organizer's history, and confirm your payment goes through a secure system. These simple steps protect your money and ensure it reaches the people who actually need help.

## Why Verification Matters for Donors

Every fake campaign that successfully collects money has two victims: the donor who loses their money, and the legitimate causes that lose potential support because trust has been eroded. Taking a moment to verify before giving protects everyone — including the real campaigners who benefit from a trustworthy ecosystem.

## The Donor's Checklist

Before you donate, go through this checklist:

### 1. Verification Status

**What to look for:** A verification badge or checkmark on the campaign page.

On Fundraising.ph, a verified campaign means:

- The organizer's identity has been confirmed with a government-issued ID
- Supporting documents (medical certificates, billing statements, etc.) have been reviewed
- The bank account for payouts matches the verified organizer
- The platform has no reason to believe the campaign is fraudulent

**How to check:** Look for the verification icon near the campaign title or organizer name. If there's no badge, the campaign may be new and awaiting review, or it may have failed verification.

### 2. Organizer Identity

**What to look for:**

- **Real name** — Does the organizer use their real name (not a pseudonym or "Admin")?
- **Profile photo** — Is there a clear profile photo of the organizer?
- **Profile history** — Does the organizer have previous campaigns or a track record?
- **Social media links** — Can you verify the organizer through connected social accounts?

**Red flags:**

- Anonymous organizer with no profile information
- Brand new account with no history
- Stock photo as profile picture
- Name that doesn't match Filipino naming conventions for a PH-specific campaign

### 3. Campaign Documents

**What to look for:**

Legitimate campaigns upload supporting documents. Check for:

| Campaign Type | Documents to Look For |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Medical | Medical certificate, hospital billing statement, doctor's letter, lab results |
| Education | Enrollment certificate, tuition assessment, student ID |
| Disaster relief | Damage photos, barangay certification, incident reports |
| Community | Project proposal, barangay permit, organizational documents |
| Church | Church authorization, project budget, photos |

**How to evaluate documents:**

- Are they on official letterhead (hospital, school, barangay)?
- Do dates make sense? (A medical certificate dated months before the campaign may be outdated)
- Are amounts mentioned in documents consistent with the campaign goal?
- Are there multiple documents supporting the story, or just one vague reference?

For more on what valid proof looks like, see [How to Show Proof to Donors](/learn/guides/how-to-show-proof-to-donors).

### 4. Story Specificity

**Genuine campaigns include:**

- Specific names of beneficiaries (not just "a family" or "a child")
- Exact location (hospital name, school name, barangay)
- Specific dates (diagnosis date, disaster date, enrollment deadline)
- Itemized cost breakdown
- Clear explanation of what happens if the goal isn't met

**Red flags:**

- Vague story with no names or locations
- Emotional language with no supporting facts
- Goal amount with no cost breakdown
- Copy-pasted text that doesn't match the photos

Read [How to Write a Compelling Fundraising Story](/learn/guides/how-to-write-fundraising-story) to understand what genuine, well-crafted campaign stories look like.

### 5. Payout Destination

**What to check:**

- Where are the funds going? The campaign should clearly state who receives the payout.
- Is the payout going to the beneficiary or an intermediary? Intermediaries are acceptable (e.g., a parent fundraising for a child) but should be transparent about the relationship.
- Is the platform's fee structure transparent? You should know what percentage of your donation goes to the beneficiary vs. platform fees.

For details on how payouts work, see [How Payouts Work in Online Fundraising](/learn/guides/how-payouts-work-online-fundraising).

### 6. Update History

**What to look for:**

- Has the organizer posted updates? Active campaigns with regular updates are more trustworthy.
- Do updates include new photos, progress reports, or receipts?
- Is the organizer responding to comments and questions?
- For campaigns that have been running for a while, do the updates match the campaign timeline?

**Red flags:**

- Campaign created weeks ago with zero updates
- Comments section disabled
- Organizer doesn't respond to questions
- Updates are generic ("Thank you donors!") without specific progress

See [How to Update Donors After Receiving Funds](/learn/guides/how-to-update-donors-after-funds) for what good updates look like.

### 7. Platform Trust Features

**What the platform provides:**

Fundraising.ph and similar platforms offer trust features:

- **Verification badges** — Confirming identity and documents
- **Secure payment processing** — Your payment details are encrypted and protected
- **Donation protection** — Fraudulent campaigns can be reported and donations refunded
- **Campaign monitoring** — Platforms actively review campaigns for red flags
- **Transparent fees** — You know exactly how your donation is allocated

**Why this matters:** Donating through a platform provides protection that direct transfers (GCash to a personal number, bank transfer to a stranger) do not.

## Donation Security

### Secure Payment Practices

When making your donation:

1. **Verify the URL** — Make sure you're on the official platform website (e.g., fundraising.ph, not fundraising-ph.com or similar)
2. **Look for HTTPS** — The URL should start with https:// and show a padlock icon
3. **Use the platform's payment system** — Don't be redirected to a different site for payment
4. **Never share your OTP or PIN** — Legitimate platforms will never ask for this
5. **Check your confirmation** — After donating, you should receive an email or on-screen confirmation with a reference number

### Payment Methods

| Method | Security Level | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Platform payment (credit/debit card) | High | Recommended — includes fraud protection |
| Platform payment (bank transfer) | High | Recommended — traceable and protected |
| Platform payment (GCash/Maya) | Medium-High | Good — convenient and protected by platform |
| Direct GCash to personal number | Low | Avoid — no platform protection |
| Direct bank transfer to individual | Low | Avoid — no verification or protection |
| Cash given in person | Medium | Acceptable only if you know the person |

### After You Donate

- **Save your receipt** — You'll receive a confirmation email or receipt. Keep it for your records.
- **Follow the campaign** — Check back for updates to see how your donation is being used.
- **Report concerns** — If you notice something suspicious after donating, report it to the platform immediately.
- **Consider the tax implications** — Donations to individuals are not tax-deductible. Donations to BIR-accredited organizations may be. Consult your accountant if this matters to you.

## Special Situations

### Disaster Relief Campaigns

During disasters, verify:

- Is the campaign endorsed by a recognized relief organization (Red Cross, DSWD, local government)?
- Are the photos from the current disaster or from a previous event?
- Is there a clear plan for how relief goods or funds will be distributed?
- Does the campaign mention coordination with local authorities?

See [Disaster Relief Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/disaster-relief-fundraising-philippines) for more guidance.

### Medical Campaigns

For medical campaigns specifically:

- Does the hospital mentioned actually exist and offer the stated treatment?
- Is the cost estimate reasonable for the procedure in the Philippines? (Research average costs online)
- Is there a doctor's letter or medical certificate from a named physician?
- Can you verify the diagnosis independently? (Not the patient's private details, but the general condition and its typical treatment costs)

See [Medical Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/medical-fundraising-philippines) for more.

### Campaigns Shared by Friends

Even when a trusted friend shares a campaign:

- Verify the campaign yourself using this checklist
- Ask your friend how they know the organizer or beneficiary
- Don't donate more than you can afford to lose, even with trusted referrals
- Remember that your friend may have been fooled too

## Common Mistakes Donors Make

1. **Donating based on emotion alone** — A heartbreaking story without proof is not enough
2. **Assuming all social media campaigns are verified** — Facebook posts with GCash numbers have no oversight
3. **Donating more than you can afford** — Give within your means, no matter how compelling the story
4. **Not checking for updates** — An abandoned campaign with no updates may indicate problems
5. **Sharing without verifying** — You could inadvertently share a fake campaign with your network
6. **Giving through direct transfers** — Bypassing platform protections leaves you with no recourse if the campaign is fraudulent

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it safe to donate online in the Philippines?

Yes, when you donate through reputable platforms with verification systems. Fundraising.ph uses secure payment processing and verifies campaigns before allowing payouts. The risk comes from donating through unsecured channels (direct GCash transfers, personal bank transfers to strangers).

### What if I donated to a campaign that turns out to be fake?

Report it to the platform immediately. On Fundraising.ph, confirmed fraudulent campaigns trigger a refund process for donors. Also report the transaction to your bank or payment provider.

### Should I donate anonymously?

Most platforms allow anonymous donations. However, providing your name and email allows the platform to contact you if there's an issue with the campaign, including potential refunds.

### How much should I donate?

There's no minimum or maximum. On Fundraising.ph, donations as small as ₱50 are accepted. Give what you can comfortably afford. Even small donations add up when many people contribute.

### Can I get a tax deduction for my donation?

Individual donations to campaigns on Fundraising.ph are generally not tax-deductible because they go to individuals, not accredited institutions. If tax deductibility is important to you, donate directly to BIR-accredited NGOs and charitable institutions instead.
