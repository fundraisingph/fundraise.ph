Medical fundraising is the most common type of online campaign in the Philippines, and for good reason — out-of-pocket healthcare spending accounts for over 50% of total health expenditure in the country. When a family member is diagnosed with cancer, needs surgery, or requires ongoing dialysis, the cost can easily exceed a year's salary. Online fundraising through platforms like Fundraising.ph gives families a way to tap into the bayanihan spirit and ask their community — and beyond — for help with life-saving medical expenses.

## Common Medical Fundraising Needs

### Cancer Treatment

Cancer is the most frequent reason for medical fundraising in the Philippines. Common campaigns include:

- **Chemotherapy** — Each cycle can cost ₱20,000-₱100,000+ depending on the type and stage
- **Radiation therapy** — Typically ₱150,000-₱300,000 for a full course
- **Surgery** — Tumor removal ranges from ₱100,000-₱500,000+
- **Targeted therapy and immunotherapy** — The most expensive, often ₱100,000-₱300,000 per session
- **Supportive medications** — Anti-nausea drugs, pain management, antibiotics

The most common cancers seen in fundraising campaigns:

| Cancer Type | Typical Total Cost | Common Age Group |
|-------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Breast cancer | ₱500,000-₱1,500,000 | 30-60 years |
| Leukemia (ALL) | ₱800,000-₱2,000,000 | Children and young adults |
| Lung cancer | ₱500,000-₱1,500,000 | 50-70 years |
| Colon cancer | ₱400,000-₱1,200,000 | 40-70 years |
| Liver cancer | ₱500,000-₱2,000,000 | 40-70 years |

### Surgery

Common surgical fundraising campaigns:

- **Cardiac surgery** — ₱500,000-₱2,000,000 (open heart surgery, valve replacement)
- **Orthopedic surgery** — ₱200,000-₱800,000 (fracture repair, joint replacement)
- **Organ transplant** — ₱1,000,000-₱3,000,000+ (kidney, liver)
- **General surgery** — ₱100,000-₱500,000 (appendectomy, hernia repair, gallbladder)

### Dialysis

Dialysis is a particularly challenging fundraising need because it's ongoing:

- **Hemodialysis** — ₱4,000-₱6,000 per session, 2-3 sessions per week
- **Peritoneal dialysis** — ₱30,000-₱50,000 per month
- **Kidney transplant** — ₱1,000,000-₱2,500,000 (one-time, but eliminates ongoing dialysis)

Many dialysis patients fundraise for a kidney transplant because it's more cost-effective long-term than years of dialysis.

### Medications

Drug costs drive many campaigns, especially for:

- **Maintenance medications** — For chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, heart disease)
- **Cancer drugs** — Particularly targeted therapies not covered by PhilHealth
- **Antibiotics** — For severe infections requiring hospitalization
- **Immunosuppressants** — For transplant patients
- **Rare disease medications** — Often not available locally or not covered by insurance

### Neonatal and Pediatric Care

- **Premature birth** — NICU stays can cost ₱10,000-₱30,000 per day
- **Congenital conditions** — Heart defects, cleft palate surgery, spina bifida
- **Childhood cancers** — Leukemia, neuroblastoma, brain tumors

## Required Documents for Medical Campaigns

### Essential Documents

Every medical campaign should include these documents:

1. **Medical Certificate or Clinical Abstract**
   - Must be on hospital or clinic letterhead
   - Include the attending physician's name and PRC license number
   - State the diagnosis, treatment plan, and prognosis

2. **Hospital Billing Statement or Cost Estimate**
   - Official document from the billing department
   - Should itemize major cost categories
   - A cost estimate is acceptable before treatment begins

3. **Doctor's Letter of Support**
   - A brief note from the attending physician confirming the need
   - Adds credibility and medical authority to the campaign

4. **Diagnostic Reports**
   - Laboratory results, imaging reports, pathology findings
   - Supports the diagnosis claimed in the campaign story

### Additional Documents by Situation

| Situation | Additional Documents |
|-----------|---------------------|
| PhilHealth claim | PhilHealth benefit eligibility letter |
| Insurance denial | Letter from insurance explaining non-coverage |
| Government hospital | PGH/PGH letter of acceptance or charity classification |
| Referral | Referral letter from primary care to specialist |
| Emergency | Emergency room admission records |

## Hospital Billing in the Philippines

### Understanding Hospital Bills

Hospital bills in the Philippines typically include these components:

- **Room and board** — ₱500/day (ward) to ₱5,000+/day (private suite)
- **Professional fees** — Doctor's charges for surgery, consultations, and daily rounds
- **Medicines and supplies** — Often the largest component
- **Laboratory and diagnostics** — Blood tests, imaging, biopsies
- **Operating room fees** — For surgical procedures
- **Equipment charges** — Ventilators, monitors, dialysis machines

### PhilHealth Coverage

PhilHealth provides basic coverage but often doesn't cover the full cost:

- **Z-Benefit Package** — Covers certain catastrophic illnesses (some cancers, childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia) up to specific amounts
- **Case rates** — Fixed coverage amounts for specific procedures
- **Typical coverage** — Usually 20-40% of total bill for most procedures

Many campaigns explicitly state that PhilHealth covered a portion and the remaining balance is what they're fundraising for.

### Hospital Assistance Programs

Most government hospitals have social service departments that provide financial assistance:

- **Philippine General Hospital (PGH)** — Has a robust charity program based on social welfare assessment
- **National Kidney and Transplant Institute (NKTI)** — Offers Zakat and charitable funds for qualified patients
- **Philippine Heart Center** — Has a social service office for financial aid
- **Regional and provincial hospitals** — Most have welfare desks

Campaigns that show they've already sought hospital assistance and government programs are more credible to donors.

## Verification for Medical Campaigns

### What Platforms Verify

Fundraising.ph verifies medical campaigns by reviewing:

1. **Identity** — Government ID of the organizer matches the patient or authorized representative
2. **Medical documents** — Doctor's name and license are verified, hospital is confirmed to exist
3. **Cost justification** — The goal amount is reasonable for the stated treatment
4. **Photos** — Campaign photos are consistent with the medical situation described

### Tips for Faster Verification

- Upload clear, legible scans of all documents
- Include the hospital letterhead and doctor's license number
- Provide the most recent billing statement
- If fundraising for someone else, include a signed authorization letter
- Respond promptly to any requests for additional information

## Tips for a Successful Medical Campaign

### 1. Write a Specific, Honest Story

Your story should answer:

- Who is the patient? (Name, age, location)
- What is the diagnosis? (Specific condition, stage if applicable)
- When was it diagnosed? (Date)
- What treatment is needed? (Specific procedure or medication)
- How much does it cost? (Itemized breakdown)
- What happens without treatment? (Medical consequences)
- What has been tried already? (PhilHealth, hospital assistance, family savings)

For writing guidance, see [How to Write a Compelling Fundraising Story](/learn/guides/how-to-write-fundraising-story).

### 2. Show Proof

Upload these at minimum:

- Medical certificate with diagnosis
- Hospital billing statement or cost estimate
- Photo of the patient (with their consent)
- Any supporting documents (lab results, referral letters)

See [How to Show Proof to Donors](/learn/guides/how-to-show-proof-to-donors) for best practices.

### 3. Break Down the Costs

Donors respond better to specific costs than to a single large number:

```
Treatment Cost Breakdown:
- Chemotherapy (6 cycles at ₱80,000 each): ₱480,000
- CT scans and lab tests: ₱65,000
- Supportive medications: ₱45,000
- Hospital room (10 days): ₱35,000
- Professional fees: ₱75,000
Total needed: ₱700,000
Already covered by PhilHealth: -₱150,000
Family contribution: -₱50,000
Remaining goal: ₱500,000
```

### 4. Post Regular Updates

Medical campaigns benefit greatly from updates:

- Treatment milestones (chemotherapy started, surgery completed)
- Medical progress (tumor shrinking, lab results improving)
- Financial updates (how much spent, receipts)
- Patient recovery (photos of the patient getting better)

See [How to Update Donors After Receiving Funds](/learn/guides/how-to-update-donors-after-funds) for templates.

### 5. Leverage Social Media

Share your campaign:

- In Facebook groups related to the medical condition
- With support communities (cancer survivors, transplant groups)
- On personal social media accounts
- Through church or community group chats
- With workplace contacts

### 6. Get Endorsements

If possible, get endorsements from:

- The patient's doctor (a brief note of support)
- The hospital's social service department
- Community leaders or local officials
- Mutual friends who can vouch for the campaign

## Dealing With Common Challenges

### When Costs Change

Medical costs often change during treatment. If costs increase:

- Be transparent about the change immediately
- Update the campaign goal if necessary
- Explain why the cost changed (complications, additional procedures, extended treatment)
- Provide updated documentation (new billing statement)

### When Treatment Takes Longer Than Expected

- Post regular updates explaining the timeline change
- Set expectations for a longer campaign
- Consider breaking the campaign into phases
- Thank donors for their patience and continued support

### When the Patient's Condition Worsens

- Share the news honestly and promptly
- Explain what the new situation means for the campaign
- Adjust the goal or purpose if needed (e.g., from "cure" to "comfort care and family support")
- Donors who have been following the story will understand and often increase their support

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I fundraise for someone else's medical expenses?

Yes. You can create a campaign for a family member, friend, or community member. You'll need to provide an authorization letter from the patient (or their guardian) and documents showing your relationship. The platform may require additional verification for third-party campaigns.

### What if PhilHealth covers part of the cost?

State this clearly in your campaign. "PhilHealth will cover approximately ₱150,000, leaving a balance of ₱550,000 that we need to raise." This actually increases credibility because it shows you've explored all options.

### Can I fundraise for ongoing treatment like dialysis?

Yes, but consider breaking it into specific time periods rather than an indefinite campaign. "Fundraising for 3 months of dialysis (₱180,000)" is more compelling than "Fundraising for dialysis" with no end point.

### Should I include the hospital name in my campaign?

Yes. Naming the specific hospital (and department if applicable) adds credibility. It allows donors to verify the institution and shows transparency.

### What if the patient doesn't want their photo online?

Respect the patient's privacy. You can use a photo that doesn't show their face, or use a photo of the medical documents instead. Include a note explaining the patient's preference for privacy.
