Churches are at the heart of Filipino community life, and fundraising for church projects is a natural extension of the congregation's shared mission. Whether you're raising funds for a new church building, a mission trip, community outreach, or youth ministry, online platforms like Fundraising.ph help congregations reach beyond their Sunday collection plate to fund their vision. This guide covers the unique considerations, compliance requirements, and strategies for successful church fundraising in the Philippines.

## The Role of Churches in Philippine Fundraising

### Why Church Fundraising Matters

The Philippines is predominantly Christian (over 85% Catholic, with significant Protestant, Evangelical, and Iglesia ni Cristo communities). Churches:

- Serve as community hubs, especially in rural areas
- Provide social services including feeding programs, disaster relief, and education
- Organize missions and outreach to underserved communities
- Maintain historic and culturally significant buildings
- Support clergy and church workers, many of whom receive modest stipends

### Church Fundraising vs. Secular Fundraising

Church fundraising has unique characteristics:

- **Built-in community** — Congregations are natural donor bases
- **Trust structures** — Church leadership provides credibility
- **Regular gathering** — Weekly services provide consistent communication channels
- **Spiritual motivation** — Donors are motivated by faith and ministry, not just charity
- **Tax-exempt status** — Many churches have BIR tax exemptions that benefit donors

## Types of Church Fundraising Campaigns

### Church Building Funds

The most common large-scale church fundraising effort:

- **New church construction** — Building a new church from the ground up (₱1,000,000-₱10,000,000+)
- **Church renovation or expansion** — Repairing, expanding, or modernizing existing structures (₱200,000-₱5,000,000)
- **Parsonage or pastor's housing** — Providing housing for clergy (₱500,000-₱3,000,000)
- **Church equipment** — Sound systems, instruments, projectors, chairs (₱50,000-₱500,000)

**What to include in your campaign:**
- Architectural plans or concept drawings
- Detailed budget breakdown (materials, labor, permits)
- Photos of the current church or building site
- Timeline with construction phases
- How many people the church serves

### Mission Trips and Evangelism

Fundraising for short-term and long-term mission work:

- **Local mission trips** — Medical missions, construction projects, children's programs in underserved areas (₱20,000-₱200,000)
- **International missions** — Travel, accommodation, and project costs for overseas mission trips (₱50,000-₱300,000)
- **Evangelism events** — Crusades, concerts, conferences, and community events (₱30,000-₱500,000)

**What to include:**
- Mission trip itinerary and objectives
- Team member list and qualifications
- Host community information
- Budget breakdown (travel, materials, food, accommodation)
- Expected impact and follow-up plan

### Community Outreach Programs

Churches often run social programs that need funding:

- **Feeding programs** — Regular meals for malnourished children, elderly, or homeless
- **Scholarship programs** — Church-sponsored education for underprivileged students
- **Medical missions** — Free medical and dental services for communities
- **Disaster relief** — Churches as first responders during calamities
- **Rehabilitation programs** — Support for recovering addicts, victims of abuse, or former inmates
- **Livelihood programs** — Skills training and micro-enterprise support

### Youth and Children's Ministry

- **Summer camps and retreats** — Subsidizing camp costs for children from low-income families
- **VBS (Vacation Bible School)** — Materials, snacks, and logistics
- **Youth group activities** — Conferences, outreach events, leadership training
- **Sunday school resources** — Teaching materials, crafts, and supplies

### Special Events and Celebrations

- **Church anniversaries** — Celebrating milestones with special projects
- **Christmas and Easter programs** — Community events during major Christian holidays
- **Church dedications** — Celebrating the completion of a building project
- **Clergy appreciation** — Honoring pastors and church workers

## Religious Organization Compliance

### Legal Requirements for Church Fundraising

#### SEC Registration

Most churches and religious organizations should be registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) as religious corporations:

- Required for opening corporate bank accounts
- Necessary for tax exemption applications
- Provides legal personality for property transactions
- Required by some platforms for organizational campaigns

#### BIR Registration and Tax Exemption

Churches can apply for tax-exempt status with the BIR:

- **Tax-exempt status** — Income from donations, tithes, and offerings used exclusively for religious, charitable, or educational purposes may be exempt from income tax
- **Donor's tax exemption** — Donations to tax-exempt religious organizations may be exempt from the 6% donor's tax
- **Requirements** — SEC registration, BIR Certificate of Registration, financial statements, and documentation of religious/charitable activities

#### Local Government Permits

- **Barangay clearance** — For church events and construction
- **Mayor's permit** — For church operations and events
- **Building permits** — For construction and renovation projects
- **Zoning compliance** — Ensuring the church location is zoned for religious use

### Solicitation Permits

Under PD 1564, organizations soliciting donations from the public may need a DSWD solicitation permit. This applies to:

- Public fundraising drives for charitable purposes
- Fundraising events open to the general public (not just congregation members)
- Campaigns that solicit from people outside the church membership

Fundraising within the congregation (Sunday collections, member pledges, internal campaigns) generally does not require a solicitation permit.

## Strategies for Successful Church Campaigns

### 1. Get Official Church Endorsement

Every church campaign should have written endorsement from:

- The senior pastor, priest, or church leader
- The church board or council (for larger projects)
- The church treasurer or finance committee

This endorsement should be visible on the campaign page.

### 2. Leverage the Congregation

The congregation is your primary supporter base:

- Announce the campaign during services
- Include campaign details in the church bulletin or newsletter
- Share the campaign in church group chats (Viber, WhatsApp, Messenger)
- Assign "campaign ambassadors" in each church ministry or small group
- Have church leaders share the campaign from their personal social media

### 3. Connect Faith and Action

Church campaigns resonate most when they connect giving to spiritual values:

- Reference Scripture that relates to generosity and community
- Frame the campaign as an extension of the church's mission
- Emphasize that giving is an act of worship, not just charity
- Share testimonies of how previous campaigns impacted lives

### 4. Involve the Youth

Young church members can be powerful campaigners:

- They're more active on social media and can reach wider networks
- Youth-led campaigns feel fresh and energetic
- Involving young people builds a culture of generosity for the future
- Youth groups can create content (videos, graphics, stories) for the campaign

### 5. Post Ministry Updates

Church donors want to see ministry impact:

- Photos from outreach events and programs
- Testimonies from people helped by church programs
- Construction progress photos for building campaigns
- Attendance and participation numbers for events
- Financial reports showing how funds were used

For general update guidance, see [How to Update Donors After Receiving Funds](/learn/guides/how-to-update-donors-after-funds).

## Multi-Church and Interdenominational Campaigns

Sometimes churches collaborate on larger campaigns:

- **Joint outreach programs** — Multiple churches serving the same community
- **Disaster relief** — Churches pooling resources for emergency response
- **Ecumenical events** — Joint worship events or community services
- **Shared facilities** — Multiple congregations sharing a building or property

For these campaigns:

- Establish a joint committee with representatives from each church
- Create a transparent fund management structure
- Use a platform like Fundraising.ph for centralized, accountable fundraising
- Document the agreement between participating churches

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does our church need SEC registration to use Fundraising.ph?

Individual members can create campaigns on behalf of their church without SEC registration. However, campaigns in the church's official name and receiving funds in a corporate bank account require SEC registration. Check with the platform for specific requirements.

### Are donations to our church campaign tax-deductible?

Donations to BIR-accredited tax-exempt religious organizations may be tax-deductible. If your church has BIR tax-exempt status, donors can request an official receipt for tax purposes. Individual campaigns by church members for personal church-related needs (like a mission trip) are generally not tax-deductible.

### Can we use Fundraising.ph alongside our regular tithes and offerings?

Absolutely. Fundraising.ph is designed for specific campaigns and projects, not for replacing regular church giving. Use it for targeted fundraising (building fund, mission trip, outreach event) while maintaining your normal collection methods for tithes and offerings.

### How do we handle accountability for church funds?

Maintain the same (or higher) financial transparency standards as your regular church finances:
- Have the church treasurer or finance committee oversee fund disbursement
- Report to the church board regularly
- Post financial updates on the campaign page
- Keep all receipts and documentation
- Consider an annual report to the congregation on all fundraising activities

### What if our church denomination has specific fundraising policies?

Always follow your denomination's guidelines for fundraising. Some denominations have specific rules about how funds can be raised, who can authorize campaigns, and how funds must be managed. Present the Fundraising.ph platform to your denominational leadership if approval is needed.
