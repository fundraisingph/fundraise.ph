Donors give to campaigns they trust, and trust comes from proof. The most successful fundraising campaigns on Fundraising.ph share clear, verifiable evidence from the very start — medical certificates, hospital bills, photos of the situation, and receipts showing how funds are spent. Showing proof isn't just about meeting platform requirements; it's about respecting the people who are choosing to help you and giving them confidence that their donation will make a real difference.

## Why Proof Matters

### For Donors

Donors want assurance that:

- The person or situation described in the campaign actually exists
- The stated need is genuine and the costs are accurate
- Funds will be used for the purpose described
- The organizer is who they claim to be

A study of online giving behavior shows that campaigns with visible documentation raise up to **4x more** than campaigns without proof.

### For Organizers

Showing proof benefits you directly:

- Faster platform verification and approval
- Higher donation conversion rates
- More shares and wider reach
- Stronger relationship with donors for future campaigns
- Protection against accusations of fraud

### For the Community

Transparent campaigns strengthen the entire fundraising ecosystem. When donors have positive experiences, they're more likely to give again — helping more people in the future.

## Documentation Best Practices

### General Principles

1. **Show, don't just tell** — A photo of a hospital bill is worth more than a paragraph describing it.
2. **Be timely** — Upload proof as soon as possible. Campaigns that launch with documentation get verified faster and receive donations sooner.
3. **Keep it current** — Use recent documents (within 30 days when possible). Outdated documents raise questions.
4. **Be complete** — Don't cherry-pick. If the total bill is ₱500,000, show the full bill, not just a page showing ₱100,000.
5. **Protect privacy** — Redact sensitive information like complete bank account numbers, but don't alter document content.

### Photo Guidelines

Photos are the most powerful form of proof. Follow these guidelines:

**Do:**
- Use clear, well-lit photos that are easy to read
- Include the subject (person, location, damage) in the photo
- Show context — the hospital entrance, the damaged home, the classroom
- Take photos from multiple angles when showing damage or conditions
- Include dates when possible (a newspaper in the frame, a timestamp)
- Get consent before posting photos of other people, especially children

**Don't:**
- Use stock photos or images from the internet
- Post graphic medical photos without warning
- Share photos that identify minors without parental consent
- Heavily filter or edit photos (basic cropping and brightness adjustments are fine)
- Reuse photos from other campaigns or events

## Medical Records

Medical campaigns require the most documentation. Here's what to share:

### Essential Medical Proof

| Document | What It Shows | Tips |
|----------|--------------|------|
| Medical certificate | Diagnosis and treatment plan | Must be on hospital letterhead with doctor's license number |
| Hospital billing statement | Costs incurred or estimated | Show the most recent statement |
| Doctor's letter | Medical necessity and urgency | Ask your doctor to write a brief note of support |
| Lab results | Confirms the diagnosis | Highlight the relevant findings |
| Prescription | Ongoing medication needs | Shows recurring costs |
| Discharge summary | Treatment completed and outcomes | For post-treatment campaigns |

### How to Present Medical Records

1. **Scan or photograph** the document clearly — all text must be readable
2. **Organize by date** — Most recent first
3. **Highlight key information** — Diagnosis, amount due, doctor's name (you can do this digitally or with a highlighter before scanning)
4. **Add a brief explanation** — Don't just upload raw documents; explain what each one shows

### Example Medical Proof Upload

```
Upload 1: "Medical Certificate from Dr. Santos, PGH Oncology 
(February 15, 2025) — Confirms diagnosis of Stage 2 breast cancer 
and recommends 6 cycles of chemotherapy"

Upload 2: "Hospital Billing Statement from PGH (February 20, 2025)
— Shows estimated total cost of ₱650,000 for complete treatment"

Upload 3: "Biopsy Report (January 28, 2025) — Pathology results 
confirming the diagnosis"
```

For more on medical campaigns specifically, see [Medical Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/medical-fundraising-philippines).

## Receipts and Financial Documentation

Showing how funds are used is critical for maintaining donor trust — especially during and after the campaign.

### When to Show Receipts

- **After significant expenses** — When you've paid a hospital bill, purchased medications, or made a tuition payment
- **In campaign updates** — Include receipts as part of your regular updates
- **Upon donor request** — Some donors may ask for specific proof; respond promptly and transparently
- **In the final report** — After the campaign ends, provide a summary with receipts

### What Receipts to Keep

- Hospital payment receipts and official receipts
- Pharmacy purchase receipts
- School payment receipts and enrollment documents
- Contractor invoices (for rebuilding projects)
- Transportation receipts (if claiming related expenses)
- Any other expense paid with campaign funds

### Creating a Financial Summary

After your campaign, create a simple financial summary:

## Education Campaign Documentation

For education campaigns, show:

- **Enrollment documents** — Certificate of enrollment, registration form
- **Tuition assessment** — Official document showing the amount due
- **School ID** — Current student identification
- **Grade reports** — Proof of good academic standing (shows the investment is worthwhile)
- **Expense receipts** — After paying tuition, show the official receipt

See [Education Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/education-fundraising-philippines) for more details.

## Disaster Relief Documentation

Disaster campaigns need visual proof above all:

- **Before and after photos** — Show the damage clearly
- **Geotagged photos** — Prove the location is real
- **Barangay or municipal certification** — Official confirmation of the disaster
- **News coverage** — Links or screenshots from legitimate news sources
- **Distribution photos** — When aid is distributed, document it

For rapid-onset disasters, initial campaigns may start with photos and social media evidence, with formal documents following later. See [Disaster Relief Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/disaster-relief-fundraising-philippines).

## Transparency Tips

### Proactive Transparency

Don't wait for donors to ask. Build trust by:

- **Posting updates regularly** — At least once a week during an active campaign
- **Sharing both good and bad news** — If costs changed or there's a setback, be honest about it
- **Acknowledging when you don't know something** — "We're still waiting for the final billing from the hospital" is better than silence
- **Thanking donors by name** (when they opt in) — Shows real people are giving
- **Responding to comments and questions** — Prompt responses build confidence

### The Transparency Standard

Hold yourself to this standard: Would a donor who gave ₱5,000 feel informed and respected by how you're communicating? If yes, you're doing it right.

### Common Transparency Mistakes

- **Disappearing after receiving funds** — The number one way to lose trust
- **Vague updates** — "Thank you for your donations, we used them" tells donors nothing
- **Hiding problems** — If treatment was delayed or costs increased, donors understand — but only if you tell them
- **Not sharing the final outcome** — Donors invested in your story; they deserve to know how it ends

## What Donors Expect

Understanding donor expectations helps you meet them:

### First-Time Donors

First-time donors to online campaigns are often nervous. They need:

- Clear evidence that the campaign is real (photos, documents)
- Platform verification badges
- Seeing that other people have already donated (social proof)
- A way to contact the organizer if they have questions

### Repeat Donors

Donors who have given to online campaigns before look for:

- Detailed updates from previous campaigns by the same organizer
- Receipts and financial breakdowns
- Consistency between the story and the documentation
- Professional communication from the organizer

For the donor's perspective, see [What Donors Should Check Before Giving Online](/learn/guides/what-donors-should-check).

## Privacy Considerations

### What to Redact

When sharing documents, redact (cover or blur):

- Complete bank account numbers (show only the last 4 digits)
- Social security numbers or SSS numbers
- Complete addresses of minors
- Contact information of medical professionals beyond their name and hospital

### What NOT to Redact

Do not redact:

- Names of patients (with consent) — Redacting names raises suspicion
- Dates and amounts on bills
- Doctor's names and license numbers
- Hospital or institution names
- Diagnosis information (the core of the proof)

### Patient Privacy

If the patient prefers privacy:

- Use first name and last initial ("Maria S.")
- Focus on the medical situation rather than personal details
- Get written consent before sharing any medical information
- Consider having a family member or authorized representative run the campaign

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to show every single receipt?

You should show receipts for all major expenses paid with campaign funds. For small expenses (under ₱500), a general description is usually sufficient. The goal is to show donors that funds were used for the stated purpose.

### What if a donor asks for proof I don't have?

Be honest. If you don't have a receipt for a specific expense, explain why and offer alternative proof. For example, if you lost a pharmacy receipt, you might ask the pharmacy for a duplicate or show the medication itself with a note explaining the situation.

### Can I post proof in Filipino/Tagalog?

Yes. Documents in Filipino are fully accepted and often preferred by local donors. If your donors are primarily Filipino-speaking, posting explanations in Filipino is natural and appropriate.

### How do I share proof without compromising privacy?

Redact sensitive identification numbers and financial account details. For medical records, you can crop the document to show only the relevant portions (diagnosis, treatment plan, amount due) while hiding personal contact information.
