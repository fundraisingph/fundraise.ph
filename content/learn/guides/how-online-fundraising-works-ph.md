Online fundraising in the Philippines lets individuals, families, and organizations collect donations through the internet — whether for medical bills, education, disaster relief, or community projects. Platforms like Fundraising.ph make it possible to create a campaign in minutes, share it through social media, and receive funds directly to your bank account. At its core, it's the digital version of *bayanihan* — the Filipino tradition of communal unity — scaled to reach supporters across the country and around the world.

## What Is Online Fundraising?

Online fundraising (also called crowdfunding or digital giving) is the practice of collecting financial contributions from multiple people via the internet. Instead of going door-to-door or hosting a single fundraising event, organizers create a campaign page that describes their cause, set a fundraising goal, and share the link through social media, messaging apps, and email.

In the Philippines, online fundraising has grown rapidly because of:

- **Widespread social media use** — The Philippines consistently ranks among the top countries for time spent on social media, making Facebook, TikTok, and X natural channels for sharing campaigns.
- **Mobile payments and e-wallets** — GCash, Maya, and online bank transfers have made donating as easy as scanning a QR code or tapping a button.
- **Strong family and community ties** — Filipinos are culturally inclined to help those in need, especially within their networks.
- **Frequent need for medical and disaster assistance** — Typhoons, earthquakes, and high out-of-pocket healthcare costs create ongoing demand for community support.

## How Online Fundraising Works

The process follows a consistent pattern across most platforms:

1. **Create a campaign** — You write your story, upload photos or documents as proof, and set a fundraising goal.
2. **Verification** — The platform reviews your identity and documents to confirm the campaign is legitimate.
3. **Share your campaign** — You share the campaign link through social media, group chats, email, and community networks.
4. **Donors contribute** — Supporters donate using credit cards, bank transfers, e-wallets, or other payment methods supported by the platform.
5. **Updates and transparency** — You post updates showing how funds are being used, building trust with donors.
6. **Payout** — Once verified, funds are transferred to your bank account according to the platform's payout schedule.

For more on how payouts work specifically, see [How Payouts Work in Online Fundraising](/learn/guides/how-payouts-work-online-fundraising).

## Legal Considerations in the Philippines

Fundraising in the Philippines is subject to certain laws and regulations:

### Solicitation Permits

Under [Presidential Decree No. 1564](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1978/pd_1564_1978.html), soliciting donations for charitable or public welfare purposes requires a permit from the **Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)** or the local social welfare office. This applies to:

- Public solicitation campaigns
- Organizations fundraising on behalf of others
- Campaigns that aim to collect from the general public

Individuals fundraising for personal causes (such as their own medical expenses) may not always need a solicitation permit, but requirements vary. Always check with your local DSWD office.

### Anti-Money Laundering (AMLA)

Large transactions may be flagged under the Anti-Money Laundering Act. Legitimate fundraising campaigns with proper documentation typically have no issues, but organizers should keep records of all donations received and how they were used.

### Tax Implications

- **Individual recipients**: Donations received for personal use (e.g., medical treatment) are generally not taxable as income.
- **Organizations**: Registered NGOs and non-profits must comply with BIR reporting requirements. Donations to accredited NGOs may be tax-deductible for the donor.
- **Platform fees**: These are typically deducted before payout and are a business expense of the platform, not the organizer.

### Data Privacy (Data Privacy Act of 2012)

Campaigns that collect personal information from donors must comply with the Data Privacy Act. Platforms like Fundraising.ph handle this on behalf of organizers, but you should never share donor information with third parties without consent.

## Bayanihan Culture and Digital Giving

*Bayanihan* is the Filipino spirit of communal unity and cooperation — traditionally symbolized by neighbors carrying a house together. Online fundraising is the modern expression of this value.

Filipinos donate for many reasons rooted in culture:

- **Family first** — Campaigns from extended family members and close friends receive the most support.
- **Religious compassion** — The predominantly Catholic culture emphasizes charity (*limos*) as a moral duty.
- **Community pride** — Barangay-level and provincial campaigns benefit from a sense of shared identity.
- **Kanya-kanyang tulong** — The idea that everyone contributes what they can, no matter how small.

This cultural foundation means that well-told, authentic stories resonate deeply with Filipino donors. For guidance on crafting your story, see [How to Write a Compelling Fundraising Story](/learn/guides/how-to-write-fundraising-story).

## Steps to Start Your First Campaign

### Step 1: Gather Your Documents

Before creating your campaign, prepare the documents that verify your need. Different campaign types require different documents:

- **Medical**: Hospital billing statements, medical certificates, doctor's letters
- **Education**: Certificate of enrollment, tuition assessment, school ID
- **Disaster relief**: Photos of damage, barangay certification, incident reports
- **Community**: Project proposal, barangay permit, organization registration

See [What Documents Do You Need to Start a Fundraising Campaign](/learn/guides/what-documents-needed-fundraising) for a complete checklist.

### Step 2: Write Your Story

Your campaign story is the most important factor in its success. Answer these questions:

- **Who** is affected? (Include names, ages, and locations when appropriate)
- **What** happened? (Describe the situation clearly)
- **Why** do you need help? (Be specific about costs)
- **How much** do you need? (Provide a breakdown)
- **How** will the funds be used? (Itemize expenses)

Read [How to Write a Compelling Fundraising Story](/learn/guides/how-to-write-fundraising-story) for detailed guidance and examples.

### Step 3: Upload Proof

Donors need to see evidence that your campaign is legitimate. Upload:

- Photos of the person, situation, or project
- Scanned documents (hospital bills, certificates, etc.)
- Any relevant identification

See [How to Show Proof to Donors](/learn/guides/how-to-show-proof-to-donors) for best practices.

### Step 4: Set a Realistic Goal

Base your goal on actual costs. Overestimating can make your campaign seem less credible, while underestimating means you may need to create a second campaign. Include:

- Direct costs (medical bills, tuition, materials)
- Related expenses (transportation, food during hospitalization, medicine)
- A small buffer for unexpected costs (5-10%)

### Step 5: Share Strategically

- Start with family and close friends — they are most likely to donate and share.
- Post on Facebook, TikTok, and other social media platforms.
- Share in relevant groups and communities.
- Send personal messages to your network.
- Update your posts regularly with progress.

### Step 6: Post Updates

Donors want to know their money is making a difference. Post updates:

- When you hit milestones (25%, 50%, 75% of goal)
- When significant events happen (surgery completed, school enrolled, supplies delivered)
- With photos showing progress
- After the campaign ends with a final report

Read [How to Update Donors After Receiving Funds](/learn/guides/how-to-update-donors-after-funds) for templates and schedules.

## The Donor's Perspective

Understanding how donors think helps you create better campaigns:

### What Donors Look For

1. **Authenticity** — Real stories with specific details, not vague appeals
2. **Proof** — Documents, photos, and evidence that back up the story
3. **Transparency** — Clear breakdown of how funds will be used
4. **Updates** — Regular progress reports after donating
5. **Verification** — Platform-verified badges that confirm legitimacy

For a donor's complete checklist, see [What Donors Should Check Before Giving Online](/learn/guides/what-donors-should-check).

### How Donors Decide to Give

Most Filipino donors follow this decision process:

1. They see a campaign shared by someone they know or trust
2. They read the story and look at the photos
3. They check if the campaign is verified by the platform
4. They look at how much has been raised and how many people have donated
5. They decide based on emotional connection and perceived legitimacy

### Common Donor Concerns

- **"Is this real?"** — Verification badges and documents address this
- **"Will my money reach the intended recipient?"** — Transparent payout information helps
- **"Is my payment secure?"** — Platform trust signals and secure payment badges matter
- **"How will I know my donation helped?"** — Regular updates resolve this

For protecting yourself as a donor, see [How to Avoid Fake Fundraising Campaigns](/learn/guides/how-to-avoid-fake-campaigns).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is online fundraising legal in the Philippines?

Yes. Online fundraising is legal. However, organizations soliciting from the public may need a DSWD solicitation permit under PD 1564. Personal campaigns for your own or a family member's needs generally do not require a permit, but it's best to verify with local authorities.

### How much does it cost to create a campaign?

On Fundraising.ph, creating a campaign is free. The platform deducts a small fee from each donation to cover payment processing and platform operations. There are no upfront costs.

### How long does it take to receive funds?

After verification, payouts are typically processed within 3-7 business days. The first payout may take longer as the platform verifies your identity and bank details. See [How Payouts Work](/learn/guides/how-payouts-work-online-fundraising) for details.

### Can I fundraise for someone else?

Yes. You can create a campaign on behalf of a family member, friend, or community. You will need to provide documentation showing the connection and the beneficiary's consent. The platform may require additional verification for third-party campaigns.

### What happens if I don't reach my goal?

Most platforms, including Fundraising.ph, release funds regardless of whether the goal is reached. You receive whatever amount was donated, minus platform fees. This is important to know — every donation counts, even if the full goal isn't met.

### Do I need to pay taxes on donations received?

Donations received for personal needs (medical, education) are generally not considered taxable income. However, if you are fundraising as an organization, different rules apply. Consult a tax professional for your specific situation.
