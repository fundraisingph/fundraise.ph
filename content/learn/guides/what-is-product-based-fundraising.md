Product-based fundraising is a method where organizations sell physical products to raise money for their cause — donors receive something tangible in return for their contribution. Instead of simply asking for donations, schools, churches, community groups, and nonprofits offer products like chocolates, baked goods, school merchandise, or artisanal items, with proceeds going toward the campaign goal. In the Philippines, this model combines the bayanihan spirit of giving with the cultural appreciation for thoughtful exchanges, making it one of the most sustainable and effective fundraising approaches available.

## What Is Product-Based Fundraising?

### Definition

Product-based fundraising (sometimes called cause commerce or social enterprise fundraising) is the practice of selling goods where a defined portion or all of the proceeds support a specific cause, project, or organization. Unlike pure donation campaigns where supporters give money without receiving anything in return, product-based fundraising creates a value exchange: supporters get a product they want, and the organization earns revenue for its mission.

This model sits at the intersection of traditional fundraising and social enterprise. It's not charity in the pure sense — it's commerce with a purpose.

### How It Differs from Donation-Based Fundraising

| Aspect | Donation-Based | Product-Based |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| What supporters give | Money with no tangible return | Money in exchange for a product |
| Motivation | Generosity, compassion, shared values | Combination of wanting the product and supporting the cause |
| Transparency | Donors need proof of fund use | The product itself is proof of value |
| Repeat support | Depends on updates and trust | Driven by product quality and desire |
| Revenue model | One-time or recurring donations | Sales revenue minus product costs |
| Best for | Emergency needs, medical, disaster | Schools, churches, community groups, ongoing programs |

### The Philippine Context

Product-based fundraising is deeply rooted in Filipino culture. Long before online platforms existed, Filipino communities raised funds through:

- **Bake sales and food sales** — A staple of PTA meetings and church events
- **Raffle tickets** — Sold at community fiestas and school events
- **Door-to-door product sales** — Students selling cookies, chocolates, or school merchandise
- **Community cookbooks** — Families contributing recipes, with proceeds going to a community project
- **Bazaar and tiangge booths** — Organizations selling products at community markets

These traditions translate naturally to online platforms like Fundraising.ph, where product-based campaigns can reach a wider audience beyond the immediate community.

## How Product-Based Fundraising Works

### The Basic Process

1. **Choose a product** — Select a product that aligns with your cause and appeals to your target supporters
2. **Set pricing and margins** — Determine the selling price, product cost, and profit margin for your organization
3. **Create the campaign** — Set up your product-based campaign on Fundraising.ph with photos, descriptions, and pricing
4. **Promote and sell** — Share the campaign through social media, community networks, and events
5. **Fulfill orders** — Deliver products to buyers
6. **Report results** — Share the total raised and how funds will be used for your cause

### Revenue Structure

```
Example: Selling Chocolates for a School Campaign

Selling price per box:        ₱250
Cost per box (from supplier): ₱150
Profit per box:               ₱100
Units sold:                   500
Total revenue:                ₱125,000
Total product cost:           ₱75,000
Total profit for the cause:   ₱50,000
```

### Pricing Considerations

Setting the right price is critical:

- **Price too low** — You won't raise enough to justify the effort
- **Price too high** — Supporters won't buy, even for a good cause
- **Know your audience** — A community barangay campaign prices differently than an exclusive school fundraiser
- **Factor in all costs** — Product cost, packaging, delivery, platform fees
- **Transparent markup** — Be clear about how much goes to the cause. Supporters appreciate knowing that "₱100 from every box goes to the school building fund"

## Who Is Product-Based Fundraising For?

### Schools and PTAs

Schools are the most common users of product-based fundraising in the Philippines:

- **PTA campaigns** — Selling products to fund classroom improvements, school events, and teacher development
- **Student council projects** — Selling school merchandise, concert tickets, or food items
- **School clubs and organizations** — Raising money for competitions, outreach programs, and activities
- **Class-specific campaigns** — Funding graduation expenses, field trips, or class projects

See [School Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/school-fundraising-philippines) for more school-specific strategies.

### Churches and Religious Organizations

Churches frequently use product-based fundraising:

- **Church merchandise** — T-shirts, mugs, bookmarks with church branding
- **Food sales after services** — Baked goods, pastries, native delicacies
- **Concert tickets** — Choir performances, worship events
- **Seasonal products** — Christmas cards, Easter items, church calendars
- **Community cookbooks** — Congregation recipe collections

See [Church Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/church-fundraising-philippines) for more.

### Community Groups and Barangays

Community organizations use product sales to fund local projects:

- **Barangay merchandise** — Shirts and items featuring local pride
- **Community food products** — Locally made products sold to raise funds for barangay projects
- **Artisan crafts** — Handmade items from community members, with proceeds supporting community initiatives

See [Community Fundraising in the Philippines](/learn/guides/community-fundraising-philippines) for more.

### Nonprofits and NGOs

Registered organizations use product-based fundraising for sustainability:

- **Advocacy merchandise** — Products that promote the organization's cause (environmental shirts, awareness bracelets)
- **Social enterprise integration** — Products made by beneficiaries as part of livelihood programs
- **Fundraising events** — Products sold at galas, benefit dinners, and awareness walks

### Businesses With a Cause

Some businesses operate on a product-based fundraising model:

- **Buy-one-give-one models** — For every product purchased, one is donated to a beneficiary
- **Percentage-of-sales donations** — A portion of each sale goes to a designated cause
- **Cause-specific product lines** — Special products created to raise funds for a specific campaign

## Examples of Product-Based Fundraising

### Serg's Chocolates Model

A well-known example in the Philippine fundraising space is the chocolate fundraising model:

- Pre-made chocolate products sold by schools and organizations
- Supporters buy boxes at a set price (e.g., ₱200-₱300 per box)
- The organization keeps a defined profit per unit sold
- No upfront cost to the organization — products are paid for from sales revenue
- Delivery is coordinated by the supplier

This model works because:
- The product is universally appealing (everyone likes chocolate)
- Pricing is accessible for most Filipino households
- The logistics are handled by the supplier
- The profit margins are clear and predictable

### School Cookie and Baked Goods Drives

Traditional school bake sales adapted for online ordering:

- Students and parents bake or source cookies, brownies, and pastries
- Orders are taken online through Fundraising.ph
- Products are delivered at school or picked up at designated locations
- All proceeds go to the school campaign

**Typical profit margins:** 50-70% (since ingredients are often donated or sponsored)

### Community Product Sales

Products made by community members:

- **Native delicacies** — Pastillas, polvoron, yema, dried mangoes
- **Handicrafts** — Woven items, handmade jewelry, home decor
- **Agricultural products** — Community-grown rice, vegetables, or processed foods
- **Custom merchandise** — Barangay shirts, event-specific items

### Holiday and Seasonal Products

Timed around Philippine holidays and celebrations:

- **Christmas products** — Cards, gift wraps, holiday baskets, lanterns (parol)
- **Valentine's items** — Flowers, chocolates, personalized cards
- **School opening supplies** — Bundled school supply packages
- **Graduation items** — Lei, flowers, graduation souvenirs
- **Fiesta merchandise** — Products celebrating local town fiestas

## Benefits of Product-Based Fundraising

### 1. Tangible Value for Supporters

Supporters receive something real in exchange for their money. This reduces the hesitation some people feel about donating because they don't feel like they're "just giving money away." They get a product they enjoy while supporting a cause.

### 2. Sustainable Revenue

Unlike one-time donations, product-based fundraising can be repeated regularly. If the quality is good, supporters look forward to buying again:

- **Annual school bake sales** become traditions
- **Seasonal products** (Christmas, graduation) create recurring demand
- **Quality products** generate word-of-mouth and repeat customers

### 3. Community Engagement

Product-based fundraising involves the community in a way that pure donations don't:

- Parents help bake and package products
- Students participate in selling and promoting
- Community members contribute their skills (cooking, crafting, designing)
- The act of selling creates conversations about the cause

### 4. Broader Reach

Not everyone is comfortable donating money to a cause, but many people are happy to buy a product — especially if they know proceeds go to a good cause. Product-based fundraising captures support from people who might not otherwise contribute.

### 5. Less Sensitivity to "Donor Fatigue"

Donors can experience fatigue from repeated requests for money. Products offer something new each time — a different item, a seasonal offering, a limited edition — which keeps supporters engaged without feeling pressured.

### 6. Skills Development

For schools and youth organizations, product-based fundraising teaches valuable skills:

- **Entrepreneurship** — Planning, pricing, marketing, and selling
- **Financial literacy** — Budgeting, tracking expenses, calculating profit
- **Teamwork** — Coordinating production, sales, and delivery
- **Communication** — Promoting the product and the cause

## Risks and Considerations

### 1. Upfront Costs and Inventory Risk

Some product-based models require upfront payment for inventory:

- **Risk** — Unsold products mean lost money
- **Mitigation** — Use pre-order models where supporters pay before you order from the supplier. This eliminates inventory risk entirely.
- **Supplier terms** — Some suppliers offer consignment (you only pay for what you sell)

### 2. Logistics and Fulfillment

Delivering products to buyers takes planning:

- **Delivery logistics** — How will products reach buyers? School pickup, barangay distribution, personal delivery?
- **Packaging** — Products must arrive in good condition
- **Timeline** — Set clear expectations about when products will be delivered
- **Volume** — Large orders require organized fulfillment systems

### 3. Quality Control

The product reflects on your organization:

- **Taste/quality matters** — A bad product hurts your reputation and future campaigns
- **Test first** — Sample the product before committing to a large order
- **Consistent quality** — Every unit should meet the same standard
- **Handle complaints professionally** — Replace defective products promptly

### 4. Pricing Sensitivity

Filipino consumers are price-sensitive:

- Research what similar products cost in the market
- Don't overprice just because it's "for a cause" — the product should offer fair value
- Be transparent about the markup and where the profit goes
- Offer multiple price points when possible (e.g., small and large sizes)

### 5. Competition With Pure Donation Campaigns

Some supporters may question why they should buy a product when they could just donate the same amount directly:

- **Answer:** Products expand your supporter base to include people who wouldn't donate otherwise
- **Strategy:** Offer both options — buy a product OR make a direct donation
- **Positioning:** Frame the product as a gift (to self or others) that also supports a cause

## Setting Up a Product-Based Campaign on Fundraising.ph

### Campaign Creation

When creating a product-based campaign:

1. **Choose "Product-Based Fundraising" as your campaign type**
2. **Upload clear product photos** — Multiple angles, packaging, and if applicable, the product being used or enjoyed
3. **Write a compelling product description** — What it is, why supporters will love it, and how their purchase helps
4. **Set clear pricing** — Show the price per unit and how much goes to the cause
5. **Define delivery options** — Pickup locations, delivery areas, and estimated timelines
6. **Set inventory limits** — If you have a fixed number of units, make this clear

### Storytelling for Product Campaigns

Even product-based campaigns need a good story. Answer these questions:

- **What cause does this product support?** — Be specific about the project or need
- **Who made or sourced the product?** — If community members made it, share their story
- **What will the funds achieve?** — "500 boxes sold = new classroom whiteboards for all Grade 4 sections"
- **Why should supporters choose this product?** — Quality, uniqueness, social impact

For storytelling guidance, see [How to Write a Compelling Fundraising Story](/learn/guides/how-to-write-fundraising-story).

## Compliance Reminders

### Business Permits

If you're selling products regularly (not a one-time campaign), you may need:

- **Barangay clearance** — For selling within the community
- **Mayor's permit** — For ongoing commercial activity
- **BIR registration** — If sales exceed the threshold for informal selling

One-time school or church fundraisers are typically exempt from business permit requirements, but check with your local government.

### Food Safety

If selling food products:

- Follow basic food safety and hygiene practices
- Include ingredient lists and allergen warnings
- Use proper packaging to prevent contamination
- Check LGU requirements for food selling permits

### Tax Implications

- **Occasional fundraising sales** — Generally not subject to business tax for schools and registered organizations
- **Regular product sales** — May be subject to percentage tax or VAT depending on volume
- **Receipts** — Issue receipts for all sales, even for fundraising purposes

## Tips for a Successful Product-Based Campaign

### 1. Choose the Right Product

| Factor | Consider |
|--------|----------|
| Appeal | Will your target supporters actually want this product? |
| Price point | Is it affordable for your community? |
| Profit margin | Does it leave enough for the cause after costs? |
| Shelf life | Will it last through the campaign and delivery period? |
| Logistics | Can you easily store, package, and deliver it? |
| Seasonality | Does it match the current season or occasion? |

### 2. Use the Pre-Order Model

Minimize risk by collecting orders and payment before purchasing inventory:

1. Launch the campaign with product descriptions and photos
2. Collect orders and payments through Fundraising.ph
3. Order from the supplier based on confirmed orders
4. Deliver products to buyers

This eliminates the risk of unsold inventory and ensures every product has a buyer.

### 3. Create a Sense of Urgency

- **Limited quantities** — "Only 200 boxes available"
- **Limited time** — "Orders accepted until [date]"
- **Seasonal relevance** — "Perfect for Christmas gifts"
- **Early bird offers** — "First 50 orders get a free gift"

### 4. Leverage Social Proof

- Share photos of people enjoying the product
- Post testimonials from early buyers
- Show a counter of products sold and funds raised
- Feature the most enthusiastic supporters (with permission)

### 5. Combine With Events

Product sales work best when combined with community events:

- Sell at school events (foundation day, sports fest, graduation)
- Set up booths at church gatherings
- Offer products at barangay fiestas and community celebrations
- Create a product launch event to generate buzz

### 6. Provide Excellent Customer Service

- Respond to inquiries promptly
- Deliver on time as promised
- Handle complaints gracefully
- Thank every buyer personally
- Follow up with impact updates showing how their purchase helped

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a lot of money to start a product-based fundraiser?

No. The pre-order model eliminates the need for upfront capital. You collect orders and payment first, then use the funds to purchase products from your supplier. Some suppliers also offer consignment arrangements for schools and organizations.

### What products sell best for fundraising in the Philippines?

Food products (chocolates, baked goods, native delicacies) consistently perform best because everyone eats and Filipino culture values sharing food. School merchandise (shirts, mugs) also sells well when there's strong school spirit. Seasonal products tied to Christmas and graduation have natural demand.

### Can we combine product-based fundraising with regular donations?

Absolutely. Many successful campaigns offer both options: supporters can buy a product OR make a direct donation. This captures both types of supporters — those who prefer receiving something tangible and those who prefer giving directly.

### How much should we mark up the product price?

A typical markup for fundraising products is 50-100% above cost. For example, if a box of chocolates costs ₱120 from the supplier, selling it at ₱200-₱250 is reasonable. Be transparent about how much goes to the cause.

### What if we have unsold products?

Plan for this possibility. Options include:
- Selling remaining stock at a discounted price after the campaign
- Donating unsold products to the beneficiary community
- Saving non-perishable items for the next campaign
- Using the pre-order model to avoid unsold inventory entirely

### Can individuals run product-based fundraising campaigns?

Yes. Individual organizers can create product-based campaigns on Fundraising.ph. You'll need the same identity verification as donation-based campaigns, plus clear product descriptions and delivery information.
