To start a legitimate and successful fundraising campaign in the Philippines, you need two types of documents: those that prove **who you are** (identity documents) and those that prove **why you need help** (supporting documents specific to your campaign type). Most platforms, including Fundraising.ph, require at least one government-issued ID and relevant supporting documents before your campaign can be fully verified and eligible for payout.

This guide covers every document you might need, organized by campaign type, so you can prepare everything before creating your campaign.

## Identity Documents

Every campaign organizer needs to provide identification. This protects donors and ensures funds reach the right person.

### Government-Issued IDs

Any of the following are accepted:

| Document | Issued By | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Passport | DFA | Most universally accepted |
| Driver's License | LTO | Must be current (not expired) |
| PhilHealth ID | PhilHealth | Widely available |
| SSS/GSIS ID | SSS/GSIS | Common for employed Filipinos |
| TIN ID | BIR | Free to obtain |
| Postal ID | PhilPost | Available at local post offices |
| National ID (PhilSys) | PSA | New but increasingly accepted |
| Voter's ID | COMELEC | May take time to verify |
| PRC License | PRC | For licensed professionals |
| Senior Citizen ID | OSCA | For senior organizers |
| barangay ID/certificate | Barangay | Useful as secondary ID |

### For Organizations

If you're fundraising on behalf of an organization, you'll need:

- **SEC Registration** — For corporations and non-stock, non-profit organizations
- **DSWD Registration** — For social welfare agencies
- **BIR Certificate of Registration** — Confirms tax compliance
- **Organizational bylaws or articles of incorporation**
- **Authorization letter** — If someone other than the head of the organization is creating the campaign
- **Board resolution** — Authorizing the fundraising activity

## Medical Campaign Documents

Medical campaigns are the most common type on Fundraising.ph. The documents required depend on the specific medical need.

### Essential Medical Documents

1. **Medical Certificate or Clinical Abstract**
   - Issued by the attending physician
   - Should include diagnosis, treatment plan, and prognosis
   - Must be on hospital or clinic letterhead with the doctor's license number

2. **Hospital Billing Statement or Cost Estimate**
   - Official statement from the hospital's billing department
   - Should itemize expected costs (surgery, room, medicines, professional fees)
   - A cost estimate is acceptable if treatment hasn't started yet

3. **Doctor's Referral or Recommendation Letter**
   - Explains why the specific treatment is necessary
   - May include urgency of the situation
   - Supports the fundraising goal amount

4. **Laboratory Results or Diagnostic Reports**
   - Blood tests, imaging results (X-ray, CT scan, MRI), biopsy results
   - Helps verify the diagnosis being claimed
   - Donors often look for these as proof of legitimacy

### Specific Medical Situations

**For Cancer Treatment:**
- Oncologist's treatment plan
- Chemotherapy or radiation schedule and cost
- Pathology/biopsy report confirming diagnosis
- Philippine General Hospital (PGH) or specialty hospital quotes if applicable

**For Surgery:**
- Surgical plan from the surgeon
- Anesthesia assessment
- Hospital room assignment and cost
- Post-operative care plan and costs

**For Dialysis:**
- Nephrologist's recommendation
- Hemodialysis schedule (typically 2-3x per week)
- Per-session cost estimate
- Duration of treatment needed

**For Medications:**
- Prescription from the attending physician
- Pharmacy price quotation
- Duration of medication needed

### Patient Consent

If you are fundraising for someone else (not yourself or your minor child):
- Written consent from the patient or their legal guardian
- Explanation of your relationship to the patient
- Authorization to receive and manage funds on their behalf

## Education Campaign Documents

### For Tuition Assistance

- **Certificate of Enrollment** or **Letter of Acceptance** — From the school confirming the student is enrolled or accepted
- **Assessment Form or Tuition Fee Schedule** — Shows the exact amount needed
- **Student ID** — Current school identification
- **Report Card or Transcript** — May be requested to show academic standing
- **Scholarship denial letter** (if applicable) — Explains why other aid isn't available

### For School Supplies or Uniforms

- **Itemized list** of needed supplies with estimated costs
- **School endorsement letter** — From a teacher, guidance counselor, or principal
- **Photos** of current conditions (if relevant)

### For School Infrastructure or Projects

- **Project proposal** — Detailed description of the project
- **Cost estimates** — From contractors or suppliers
- **Barangay or school authorization** — Written approval from relevant authorities
- **Site photos** — Current state of the facility

## Disaster Relief Campaign Documents

Disaster relief campaigns often need to be set up quickly, so verification requirements may be streamlined.

### Standard Requirements

- **Photos or videos** of the damage or affected area
- **Barangay certificate** — Confirming the disaster event and affected residents
- **Incident report** — From local DRRMO (Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office)
- **NDRRMC advisory** — If available, for major disasters

### For Rapid Verification

During active disasters, Fundraising.ph may accept:
- Geotagged photos showing the location
- News coverage of the event
- Social media posts from verified news sources
- Testimonials from local officials

Full documentation can be submitted after initial campaign approval.

## Community Project Documents

### Barangay or Community Projects

- **Barangay Resolution** — Approving the project
- **Project proposal** — With timeline, budget, and expected outcomes
- **Cost estimates** — From at least two suppliers when possible
- **Barangay Clearance** — For the organizer

### Cooperative or Organization Projects

- **Cooperative registration** — From CDA (Cooperative Development Authority)
- **Organizational documents** — Bylaws, membership list
- **Project feasibility study** — For larger projects
- **Financial statements** — If the organization has existing operations

## Church and Religious Organization Documents

Common requirements include:
- **Church registration** — SEC registration for the religious corporation
- **Authorization from church leadership** — Pastor, priest, or church board
- **Project proposal** — For building funds or outreach programs
- **Photos** — Current state of the church or project site
- **Budget breakdown** — Detailed cost estimates

## Solicitation Permits

### When You Need a DSWD Solicitation Permit

Under Presidential Decree No. 1564, you need a solicitation permit if:

- You are an **organization** soliciting donations from the public
- You are raising funds **for charitable or public welfare purposes**
- You are conducting door-to-door, street, or public solicitation
- You are running a fundraising event open to the general public

### When You May Not Need One

- **Personal campaigns** — Fundraising for your own or your immediate family's needs
- **Private collections** — Asking only from people you personally know (not the general public)
- **Internal organizational fundraising** — Asking only from members of your own organization

### How to Get a Solicitation Permit

1. Visit the nearest DSWD Field Office or your local social welfare office
2. Submit the required documents:
   - Organizational documents (SEC registration, bylaws)
   - Project proposal or campaign plan
   - Financial statements (if applicable)
   - Authorization letters for solicitors
   - IDs of officers and solicitors
3. Pay the processing fee (varies by region)
4. Wait for approval (typically 5-15 business days)

## Bank Account Requirements

To receive payouts from your campaign, you need a bank account in your name (or your organization's name).

### For Individual Organizers

- Active bank account in your name
- Bank account number and bank name
- Some platforms may require a bank statement or deposit slip to verify the account

### For Organization Accounts

- Corporate bank account in the organization's name
- Board resolution authorizing signatories
- SEC registration matching the bank account name

### Accepted Banks on Fundraising.ph

Most major Philippine banks are supported, including BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Landbank, DBP, PNB, Security Bank, UnionBank, Chinabank, and digital banks like Maya Bank and Gotyme. GCash and Maya wallet payouts may also be available.

## Tips for Document Preparation

1. **Prepare documents before creating your campaign** — Having everything ready speeds up verification.
2. **Use clear, legible scans or photos** — Blurry documents may be rejected.
3. **Keep originals** — Digital copies are fine for the platform, but keep physical originals.
4. **Redact sensitive information** — You may black out account numbers on bank statements, but do not alter documents.
5. **Translate if necessary** — If documents are in a local language or dialect, provide an English translation.
6. **Get documents notarized for large campaigns** — While not always required, notarized documents add credibility for high-goal campaigns.
7. **Update expired documents** — Make sure medical certificates and billing statements are recent (within 30 days when possible).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What if I don't have a government ID?

You can apply for a National ID (PhilSys) for free at any registration center. A barangay certificate can serve as a temporary alternative while you wait for your government ID. Some platforms may accept a barangay certificate combined with other supporting documents.

### Can I use someone else's bank account?

Most platforms require the bank account to be in the organizer's name. If the beneficiary is different from the organizer, additional documentation will be needed, including authorization from the beneficiary. Fundraising.ph handles this on a case-by-case basis.

### Do I need to notarize my documents?

Notarization is not required for most campaigns. However, for very large campaigns or organizational fundraising, notarized documents add an extra layer of credibility.

### How long does document verification take?

Fundraising.ph typically verifies documents within 1-3 business days. Incomplete or unclear documents may delay the process. Disaster relief campaigns may receive expedited verification.

### What if my documents are in Filipino or a local language?

Documents in Filipino are accepted. Documents in other Philippine languages should be accompanied by an English translation. The translation does not need to be certified but should be accurate.
