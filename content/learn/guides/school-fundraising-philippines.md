School fundraising in the Philippines supports everything from PTA projects and student council activities to large-scale campus improvement campaigns. Whether you're a parent, teacher, student, or school administrator, platforms like Fundraising.ph provide a modern way to raise funds that goes beyond the traditional raffle tickets and benefit concerts. This guide covers the types of school fundraising, legal considerations, and strategies for running successful school campaigns.

## Why Schools Fundraise

### The Funding Gap

Even with government support through DepEd and CHED, Philippine schools face significant funding gaps:

- **Public K-12 schools** — Government funding covers basic operations but not facility improvements, technology upgrades, or extracurricular programs
- **State universities and colleges** — Tuition is free under UAQTEA, but miscellaneous fees, laboratory fees, and living expenses remain
- **Private schools** — Parents bear the full cost, and fundraising helps offset expenses for school improvement
- **Special education** — SPED programs often need specialized equipment and materials beyond standard budgets

### What Schools Raise Funds For

- Classroom improvements (whiteboards, air conditioning, electrical work)
- Computer laboratories and internet connectivity
- Libraries and reading materials
- Science laboratory equipment
- Sports equipment and uniforms
- School events (foundation day, graduation, field trips)
- Scholarships for underprivileged students
- Teacher training and development
- School repairs and maintenance
- Emergency preparedness (first aid kits, fire extinguishers)

## Types of School Fundraising Campaigns

### PTA Fundraising

The Parent-Teacher Association is the most common organizer of school fundraising:

**Typical PTA campaigns:**
- **Classroom improvements** — Air conditioning, new desks, painting, electrical upgrades
- **School events** — Foundation day, sports fest, graduation ceremonies
- **Teacher appreciation** — Gifts, meals, and recognition events for teachers
- **Emergency needs** — Roof repairs after typhoon damage, broken water systems

**Goal range:** ₱20,000 to ₱500,000

**Required documents:**
- PTA resolution authorizing the fundraising campaign
- Project proposal with budget breakdown
- School administration endorsement (principal or school head)
- Photos of current conditions or needs
- PTA registration with the school or DepEd

**Best practices:**
- Have the PTA president and school principal co-authorize the campaign
- Share the campaign in class Viber/WhatsApp groups
- Include all parents in the communication plan
- Provide regular updates at PTA meetings and through class group chats
- Present the final financial report at a PTA meeting

### Student Council and Organization Campaigns

Student-led fundraising for school activities and causes:

**Common campaigns:**
- **Student council projects** — School events, leadership programs, student welfare initiatives
- **Club and organization activities** — Debate tournaments, science fairs, art exhibits, sports competitions
- **Outreach programs** — Student-led community service and donation drives
- **Competition travel** — Funding for students representing the school in regional or national competitions
- **Student publications** — School newspaper, yearbook, literary magazine

**Goal range:** ₱5,000 to ₱200,000

**Required documents:**
- Student organization resolution or project proposal
- Faculty adviser endorsement
- School administration approval
- Detailed budget and timeline

**Tips for student campaigns:**
- Student campaigns resonate well with alumni networks
- Use social media heavily — students are the most connected demographic
- Create engaging content (videos, graphics, stories)
- Involve the entire student body in sharing and supporting
- Show the direct benefit to students and the school community

### School Supply Drives

Campaigns to provide materials for students or classrooms:

- **Individual student supplies** — Notebooks, pens, bags, uniforms for underprivileged students
- **Classroom supplies** — Books, teaching aids, art materials, science kits
- **Technology** — Computers, tablets, printers, projectors
- **Library materials** — Books, reference materials, digital subscriptions

**Goal range:** ₱10,000 to ₱300,000

**Tips:**
- Provide a specific list of needed items with quantities and prices
- Partner with school bookstores or suppliers for bulk discounts
- Include the school's endorsement to show legitimacy
- Post photos of students receiving and using the supplies
- Share impact stories — how the supplies improved learning

### Infrastructure and Capital Campaigns

Large-scale campaigns for major school improvements:

- **New classrooms or buildings** — ₱500,000 to ₱5,000,000+
- **School renovation** — ₱200,000 to ₱2,000,000
- **Sports facilities** — Courts, tracks, equipment rooms
- **Laboratories** — Science labs, computer labs, culinary kitchens
- **School vehicles** — Buses, service vehicles for school trips

**Goal range:** ₱200,000 to ₱5,000,000+

**Required documents:**
- School board or DepEd approval (for public schools)
- Architectural plans or engineering assessments
- Detailed construction budget with contractor quotes
- School registration and accreditation documents
- Project timeline with milestones

### Product-Based School Fundraising

Combining traditional product sales with online fundraising:

| Product | Typical Profit | Best For |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| School merchandise (shirts, mugs) | Medium | Building school spirit |
| Concert tickets (school choir, band) | Medium-High | Showcasing talent |
| Food products (baked goods, pastillas) | Medium | Quick turnaround |
| Calendars (school photos, artwork) | Low-Medium | Annual tradition |
| Raffle tickets (with prizes donated) | High | Community events |
| Christmas items (cards, décor) | Medium | Holiday season |

**Integrating with Fundraising.ph:**
- Use the platform to accept pre-orders and payments
- Offer products as thank-you gifts for donations
- Track orders and distribution through the campaign page
- Accept donations from alumni and supporters who can't attend in person

## Compliance for School Campaigns

### DepEd and CHED Requirements

**Public Schools (K-12):**
- PTA fundraising must comply with DepEd Order No. 43, s. 2014 (PTA guidelines)
- Voluntary contributions only — no mandatory fees beyond approved fees
- PTA must be registered and in good standing
- Fund usage must be reported to the school and PTA members
- School principal must be informed of and endorse fundraising activities

**Public Higher Education (SUCs):**
- Student organization fundraising must comply with the school's student affairs guidelines
- University administration must approve major campaigns
- Student council funds are subject to school auditing requirements

**Private Schools:**
- Must follow the school's own fundraising policies
- School administration approval is typically required
- Tax-exempt schools must ensure fundraising complies with BIR requirements
- Collections must be properly receipted and accounted for

### Financial Transparency

All school fundraising should maintain:

- **Written fund usage plan** — Shared with all contributors before donations start
- **Receipt documentation** — Every expense documented with official receipts
- **Regular financial updates** — Posted on the campaign page and shared with the school community
- **Final financial report** — Presented to the PTA, school administration, and donors
- **Open books** — Available for review by any contributor upon request

### Child Protection

When fundraising involves or benefits children:

- Never use children's photos without parental consent
- Don't share children's full names or personal details
- Don't use images that could be considered exploitative
- Follow the school's child protection policy
- Ensure all campaigns are supervised by adults

## Strategies for Success

### Engage the Entire School Community

- **Parents** — Through PTA meetings, class group chats, and direct communication
- **Teachers** — As advocates who can promote the campaign to their networks
- **Students** — As content creators, sharers, and ambassadors
- **Alumni** — Through alumni associations and social media groups
- **Local businesses** — Sponsorships, matching donations, or in-kind contributions
- **Local government** — Endorsements, matching grants, or partnerships

### Leverage Alumni Networks

Alumni are powerful supporters of school campaigns:

- Create an alumni-specific message in the campaign
- Tag the school's alumni association in social media posts
- Share the campaign in alumni Facebook groups
- Appeal to alumni nostalgia — "Remember the old library? Help us build a new one for the next generation."
- Offer recognition (donor wall, program acknowledgment) for significant alumni contributions

### Use Events to Drive Donations

Tie fundraising to school events:

- Launch the campaign during a school event (foundation day, graduation, sports fest)
- Set up a booth at school events where people can donate on the spot
- Use school performances as fundraising opportunities
- Create a "giving day" challenge — matching donations within a specific timeframe

### Share the Impact

Donors respond to visible impact:

- Before and after photos (old classroom vs. renovated classroom)
- Testimonials from teachers and students
- Videos showing the school and the improvements
- Stories of individual students who benefited
- Attendance or enrollment numbers that show the school's reach

For more on effective storytelling, see [How to Write a Compelling Fundraising Story](/learn/guides/how-to-write-fundraising-story).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a student create a fundraising campaign for their school?

Students can create campaigns for school-related needs (competition travel, club projects, personal education costs). For campaigns representing the school or PTA, a faculty adviser or PTA officer should be the organizer or co-organizer.

### Do we need a solicitation permit for school fundraising?

PTA and school fundraising from the school community (parents, teachers, alumni) typically doesn't require a DSWD solicitation permit. If you're soliciting from the general public (through social media, public events, or door-to-door), a permit may be required. Check with your local DSWD office.

### Can multiple classes or year levels run simultaneous campaigns?

Yes, but coordinate with the school administration to avoid donor fatigue. Consider combining similar campaigns (e.g., all Grade 7 sections contributing to one science lab improvement campaign) rather than multiple small campaigns for similar purposes.

### How do we handle funds for a public school?

Funds for public school projects should go through the PTA's official bank account, not an individual's personal account. The PTA should have a treasurer who manages the funds with proper documentation and reporting.

### What if a donor wants to remain anonymous?

Respect the donor's preference. Fundraising.ph allows anonymous donations. You can acknowledge the donation in aggregate ("We received a generous anonymous donation of ₱10,000") without naming the donor.
