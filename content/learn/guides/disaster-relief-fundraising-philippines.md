The Philippines is one of the most disaster-prone countries in the world, experiencing an average of 20 typhoons per year plus earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and floods. When disaster strikes, the Filipino bayanihan spirit kicks in — and online fundraising has become one of the fastest ways to mobilize help. This guide covers how to create, verify, and manage disaster relief campaigns on Fundraising.ph, whether you're helping your own community or raising funds for affected families.

## The Philippine Disaster Context

### Why Disaster Fundraising Is Different

Disaster relief campaigns have unique characteristics compared to other fundraising types:

- **Urgency** — People need help immediately, not in weeks
- **Scale** — Hundreds or thousands of families may be affected
- **Emotional intensity** — Donors are often responding to dramatic news coverage
- **Verification challenges** — Normal documentation may be impossible in the immediate aftermath
- **Scrutiny** — Disaster campaigns attract more attention and more skepticism

### Common Disasters That Drive Fundraising

| Disaster Type | Frequency | Typical Fundraising Need |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| Typhoons (bagyo) | 20 per year, 5-8 severe | Shelter, food, water, clothing, rebuilding |
| Flooding (baha) | Annual in many areas | Evacuation supplies, home repair, appliances |
| Earthquakes (lindol) | Several per year | Structural assessment, temporary shelter, rebuilding |
| Volcanic eruptions | Periodic | Evacuation, respiratory supplies, livestock relocation |
| Fires (sunog) | Frequent in urban areas | Temporary shelter, clothing, essential items |
| Landslides | During rainy season | Rescue equipment, relocation, rebuilding |

## Setting Up a Disaster Relief Campaign

### Speed vs. Verification

In the immediate aftermath of a disaster, Fundraising.ph balances speed with security:

**Phase 1: Emergency (First 24-72 hours)**
- Campaigns can be created quickly with minimal initial documentation
- Photos of the disaster and geotagged images serve as initial verification
- Payouts may be expedited for verified organizers
- Platform monitors for duplicate or suspicious campaigns

**Phase 2: Stabilization (Days 3-14)**
- Full documentation is submitted (barangay certificates, damage assessments)
- Fund usage plan is posted
- Regular updates with distribution photos
- Coordination with local government or relief organizations is documented

**Phase 3: Recovery (Weeks to months)**
- Detailed financial reports with receipts
- Rebuilding progress updates
- Long-term recovery plan shared
- Final report with full transparency

### Required Documentation

#### Immediate (Phase 1)
- Photos or videos of the damage (geotagged if possible)
- Your location and the specific area affected
- Description of the affected community (number of families, current conditions)
- Your connection to the affected community

#### Follow-Up (Phase 2)
- Barangay certificate confirming the disaster event
- DRRMO (Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office) incident report
- List of affected families (for community-level campaigns)
- Coordination plan with local government or relief agencies

#### Recovery (Phase 3)
- Distribution records showing who received aid
- Receipts for all purchased relief goods
- Photos of relief distribution
- Financial summary of funds received and spent

For general document guidance, see [What Documents Do You Need to Start a Fundraising Campaign](/learn/guides/what-documents-needed-fundraising).

## Types of Disaster Relief Campaigns

### Individual/Family Relief

Helping one family or a small group:

- **Home destruction** — Fundraising for rebuilding or temporary shelter
- **Loss of belongings** — Replacing essential items (clothing, kitchen supplies, beds)
- **Lost income** — Supporting a family whose livelihood was destroyed
- **Medical needs from disaster** — Injuries, waterborne diseases, psychological support

**Goal range:** ₱20,000 to ₱200,000

### Community-Level Relief

Helping an entire barangay or community:

- **Evacuation center supplies** — Food, water, blankets, hygiene kits
- **Community kitchen** — Setting up a communal feeding program
- **Temporary shelter** — tents, tarps, or communal shelter materials
- **Water and sanitation** — Clean water supply, portable toilets, water purification

**Goal range:** ₱50,000 to ₱500,000

### Rebuilding Projects

Longer-term campaigns for reconstruction:

- **Home rebuilding** — Materials and labor for constructing new homes
- **School repair** — Fixing damaged classrooms and replacing supplies
- **Livelihood restoration** — Replacing fishing boats, farming equipment, or sari-sari store inventory
- **Community infrastructure** — Roads, bridges, water systems

**Goal range:** ₱100,000 to ₱1,000,000+

## Transparency in Disaster Relief

### Why Transparency Matters More in Disasters

Disaster campaigns receive intense public scrutiny because:

- Large amounts of money are raised quickly
- Multiple campaigns may address the same disaster
- News coverage draws attention to fundraising efforts
- Past incidents of disaster relief fraud have made the public cautious
- Donors are emotionally invested and expect accountability

### Best Practices for Transparent Disaster Campaigns

1. **Post a clear fund usage plan before donations start**
   - Itemized list of what you'll buy and estimated costs
   - Number of beneficiaries and how they were identified
   - Distribution plan and timeline

2. **Document every distribution**
   - Photos of goods being received by beneficiaries
   - Signed acknowledgment sheets (for organized distributions)
   - Dates and locations of each distribution

3. **Post financial updates regularly**
   - How much has been received
   - How much has been spent and on what
   - How much remains and what it's allocated for

4. **Be honest about challenges**
   - If roads are blocked and distribution is delayed, say so
   - If prices are higher than expected, explain why
   - If some families were missed, acknowledge it and explain the plan to reach them

5. **Coordinate with established organizations**
   - Working with the barangay, LGU, DSWD, or recognized NGOs adds credibility
   - Mention these partnerships in your campaign
   - Share endorsement letters or coordination messages

See [How to Show Proof to Donors](/learn/guides/how-to-show-proof-to-donors) for general transparency best practices.

## How to Verify Disaster Campaigns (For Donors)

Donors should be especially careful with disaster campaigns. Here's what to check:

### Legitimacy Indicators

- **Specific location** — The campaign names the exact barangay, municipality, or province
- **Local organizer** — The organizer is from or connected to the affected area
- **Photos match the news** — The damage shown is consistent with news reports of the disaster
- **Reasonable goal** — The amount requested makes sense for the scale of help described
- **Distribution plan** — There's a clear plan for how aid will reach beneficiaries
- **Updates** — The organizer posts regular updates with evidence of distribution

### Red Flags Specific to Disaster Campaigns

- Using photos from previous disasters or other countries
- Vague location ("typhoon victims in the Philippines") instead of specific areas
- No connection to the affected community
- Goal amount vastly exceeds realistic costs for the stated purpose
- No distribution plan or timeline
- Organizer has no verifiable local presence

For more donor safety tips, see [How to Avoid Fake Fundraising Campaigns](/learn/guides/how-to-avoid-fake-campaigns) and [What Donors Should Check Before Giving Online](/learn/guides/what-donors-should-check).

## Rapid Verification for Emergencies

### How Fundraising.ph Handles Emergency Verification

During major disasters, Fundraising.ph activates emergency protocols:

1. **Priority review queue** — Disaster campaigns are reviewed first
2. **Expedited identity verification** — Faster document review
3. **Accelerated payouts** — Funds reach affected communities sooner
4. **Dedicated support** — Staff focused on disaster campaign assistance
5. **Community verification** — Cross-referencing with news reports, DRRMO data, and social media

### What Organizers Can Do to Speed Up Verification

- Provide clear, geotagged photos of the affected area
- Include your barangay and municipality in the campaign details
- Upload any available documentation immediately
- Respond promptly to verification requests
- If you have previous campaign history on the platform, reference it

## Sample Disaster Relief Campaign Structure

### Campaign Title

"Help [Barangay Name] Recover from Typhoon [Name] — [Municipality], [Province]"

### Story Structure

```
[Opening: What happened, when, and where]

"In the early hours of [date], Typhoon [name] made landfall in 
[Municipality], [Province]. Winds of [speed] km/h and heavy rains 
devastated Barangay [name], where [number] families lost their homes."

[Impact: Who is affected and how badly]

"The barangay reports [number] homes totally damaged and [number] 
partially damaged. [Number] families are currently in the evacuation 
center at [location]. Most families lost their belongings, food 
supplies, and livelihood equipment."

[Your connection: Why you're organizing this]

"I am [name], a resident of Barangay [name] and currently serving 
as [role/position]. I'm organizing this campaign with the support 
of Barangay Captain [name] to provide immediate relief to the 
affected families."

[The plan: What you'll do with the funds]

"With [amount], we plan to provide:
- Relief packs for [number] families (rice, canned goods, water): ₱[amount]
- Hygiene kits for [number] families: ₱[amount]
- Temporary shelter materials (tarps, nails, plywood): ₱[amount]
- School supplies for affected children: ₱[amount]
Total needed: ₱[amount]"

[Proof and verification: What you've uploaded]

"I've uploaded photos of the damage, the barangay certification 
confirming the disaster, and a letter from the Barangay Captain 
endorsing this campaign."

[Call to action]

"Every donation helps. ₱500 provides a relief pack for one family. 
₱2,000 covers shelter materials. If you can't donate, please share 
this campaign to help us reach more people."
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How quickly can I start a disaster relief campaign?

You can create a campaign on Fundraising.ph within minutes. Emergency verification is prioritized, and initial campaigns can launch with photos and a basic description while full documentation follows within 48-72 hours.

### Can I fundraise for a disaster I'm not directly affected by?

Yes, but transparency is critical. Clearly state your connection to the affected community (you're from that province, you have family there, you're working with a local organization, etc.) and explain how you'll ensure funds reach the beneficiaries.

### What if I raise more than needed?

Explain your plan for excess funds upfront: "If we raise more than the target, additional funds will go toward [rebuilding/replacing livelihood equipment/assisting neighboring barangays]." Never redirect disaster funds to unrelated purposes without donor communication.

### Should I partner with an established NGO?

It's not required, but partnering with a recognized organization (Red Cross, DSWD, local church, or LGU) adds credibility and can help with distribution logistics. Mention any partnerships in your campaign.

### How do I handle relief distribution fairly?

- Work with the barangay to get an accurate list of affected families
- Use a systematic distribution process (organized by purok or household)
- Document every distribution with photos and acknowledgment
- Prioritize the most vulnerable (elderly, persons with disabilities, families with young children)
- Post updates showing the distribution process
